    Anthology series

    An anthology series groups stories under a shared series identity while narrative continuity may occur at episode or season level.

    An anthology series gives distinct stories a shared television identity. What continues can be the format, theme or presentation rather than the same characters and plot.

    There are different levels of narrative continuity:

    Pattern What remains connected Where the story normally develops
    Episode anthology Series format or theme Within individual episodes
    Season anthology Series identity and presentation Across the episodes of one season
    Continuing serial Series and continuing narrative Across seasons as well as episodes

    These are descriptive patterns, not content-type settings. A catalogue still needs to identify the actual series and its children.

    A season title can name a story

    Imagine the fictional anthology Coastal Lives. Season 1, The Lighthouse, tells one six-episode story. Season 2, The Harbour, has a different cast and tells another eight-episode story.

    The season titles communicate narrative scope. They do not necessarily establish two unrelated television series. If the chosen catalogue represents both under Coastal Lives, retaining that series identity makes its season relationships and provider matching coherent.

    A personal collection can bring together favourite anthology stories across shows without changing their original episode identities. That is a collection relationship, not a reason to assign every selected episode to a fabricated common series.

    Independence does not erase order

    For an episode anthology, many episodes may be understandable in isolation. Release order still supplies their catalogue coordinates. Selecting a different story because the number was interpreted under the wrong order remains a metadata error.

    For a season anthology, the within-season order can be essential even when seasons can be watched independently. “The seasons are unrelated” does not imply “every episode is interchangeable.”

    Emby's TV Naming documentation describes the series/season context used to recognize episodic resources. The relevant catalogue evidence determines whether a named story is a season, an episode, a separate series or a related film. Narrative similarity alone cannot select that entity type.
