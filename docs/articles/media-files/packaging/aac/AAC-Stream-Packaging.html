    AAC Stream Packaging

    Two files can contain the same AAC-encoded sound and still require different input handling. AAC is the compression family; its packaging tells a reader where encoded units begin and how to configure the decoder.

    The decoder needs information such as the audio object type, sample rate, and channel configuration. That information can be carried in transport headers or in a containing file's track description.

    Packaging Where a reader finds structure and configuration
    ADTS Repeated transport headers around encoded audio
    MP4/M4A Container sample descriptions, timing, and stored decoder configuration
    LATM A multiplex structure carrying audio and configuration information

    Those choices do not establish whether the audio is AAC-LC, HE-AAC, or another member of the family. Some coding tools and configurations need signalling beyond the basic fields in a particular wrapper.

    Copying the sound can still change bytes

    Moving AAC from ADTS into MP4 commonly removes the repeated ADTS headers and creates an AudioSpecificConfig in the container. This is a bitstream-packaging adjustment that can preserve the encoded audio without decoding and recompressing it.

    The resulting files will not be byte-for-byte identical, and their total sizes may differ. That difference is not by itself evidence of a quality change. Compare the encoded audio and configuration rather than treating container overhead as extra sound information.

    For an Emby source that is identified as AAC but behaves differently from another AAC source, check both the coding variant and the wrapper. A suitable decoder is insufficient if the input stage cannot understand the framing or discover the required configuration.

    Use inspection information that includes the container and profile, not only the word AAC. This avoids confusing a transport problem with a need to re-encode the soundtrack.
