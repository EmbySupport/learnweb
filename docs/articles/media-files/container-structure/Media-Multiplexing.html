    Multiplexing and Demultiplexing

    Picture, sound, and subtitles are separate sequences of data. Multiplexing, usually shortened to muxing, combines them into a container with track descriptions and timing. Demultiplexing, or demuxing, reads that structure and routes each component to the appropriate decoder or renderer.

    The packet sequence is illustrative. Container packets are not network packets, and track timing need not be evenly spaced.

    Interleaving places portions of related tracks near one another. A player can then obtain upcoming picture and sound without jumping across large sections of a file. It does not change how either stream was compressed.

    What a remux preserves

    A remux demultiplexes one source and multiplexes selected encoded streams into another output. It can change track order, container headers, indexing, and metadata while preserving encoded media.

    That is why a remux can finish much faster than re-encoding and can preserve picture quality. However, the destination needs a valid mapping for each codec. Some formats require different headers or bitstream framing, and some metadata may not have an equivalent destination field.

    Emby can use this kind of repackaging in a Direct Stream session. If the video is copied but the audio is converted, the overall job combines remuxing with audio transcoding.

    When comparing before and after, inspect track count, languages, and duration as well as codecs. Preserving the main video alone does not prove that every soundtrack, subtitle, chapter, or attachment survived.
