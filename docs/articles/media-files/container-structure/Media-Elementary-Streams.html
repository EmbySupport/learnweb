    Elementary Streams

    An elementary stream is one encoded component, such as a sequence of H.264 video data or AAC audio data. It is the material a container packages, rather than the complete movie presentation.

    The word elementary does not mean simple to decode. The stream can contain sophisticated compression, reference pictures, and codec-specific headers.

    What can be missing outside the container

    A raw video stream can describe the pictures without carrying the same presentation timeline, chapter list, language tags, or track-selection information as a container file. Some timing and configuration may be available inside the codec data, but the available information depends on the format.

    That distinction matters when an extracted video stream plays too quickly or loses synchronization after being combined with audio. The original container may have supplied timing that extraction did not preserve.

    A packetized elementary stream (PES) adds a systems-layer wrapper around elementary-stream data in MPEG systems. That wrapper can carry timestamps; it is still a different layer from the smaller transport-stream packets that deliver it.

    For an Emby library, keeping a suitable container is usually the more useful arrangement because it carries the presentation as a whole. Extracting a raw stream can be helpful for technical diagnosis, but it is not a general compatibility fix.

    Even copying encoded data between containers can require framing changes. For example, H.264 units can be represented with start codes or with lengths, depending on their packaging. Changing that framing is not the same as recompressing the picture.
