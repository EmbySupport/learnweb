    Damaged and Incomplete Media Files

    A file can contain gigabytes of picture data and still fail to open if essential structure is missing. An interrupted recording may never have written its final index or track tables. Corruption in the encoded media is a different kind of damage.

    What is missing or damaged? Typical consequence
    Navigation index Playback may work, while seeking becomes slow or unreliable
    Essential track configuration or sample tables The reader may be unable to interpret otherwise present media
    Encoded packets Visible artifacts, silence, decode errors, or gaps at particular moments

    These are clues, not diagnoses from a single error message.

    Why unfinished files behave differently

    Some recording arrangements carry enough information in successive fragments or packets to leave earlier portions readable after an interruption. Others depend heavily on structures completed at the end. Recoverability depends on both the container and how it was written.

    A remux can rebuild some structure when a reader can still recover the streams. It cannot recreate pictures or sound whose bytes were never recorded. A missing MP4 moov box may require recovery information that ordinary remuxing cannot supply.

    When an Emby item fails, first check that the file is complete and readable from the server's storage. Then compare probe results and errors near the failing position. An unsupported codec can produce a playback failure even when the file is intact.

    Preserve the original and attempt repairs on a copy. If the source is still available, a clean replacement is often more reliable than a partial recovery whose duration, tracks, or seeking behavior remain uncertain.
