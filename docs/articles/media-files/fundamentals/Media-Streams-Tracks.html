    Streams and Tracks

    A film with two audio languages usually stores two audio tracks alongside one video track. Selecting another language changes which audio sequence is played; it does not require a second copy of the film's picture.

    In file discussions, stream and track often refer to the same component. Track emphasizes its place in the container; stream emphasizes the sequence of media data read from it. A network stream is a broader use of the word and may deliver an entire container with several tracks.

    Example file component What selecting it changes
    Main video The moving picture
    Original-language audio The dialogue and soundtrack
    Commentary audio An alternative soundtrack
    Subtitle track Text or images displayed over the picture

    One audio track can contain several audio channels, such as a 5.1 soundtrack. Six channels are not six selectable audio tracks.

    Why the track list matters

    An Emby playback decision depends on the selected components. A compatible video track may play directly with one audio choice but need audio conversion with another. Choosing an image-based subtitle track can also change the work required to display it.

    Track numbers help inspection tools distinguish components, but they are not durable identities across editing. A remux that adds or removes tracks may reorder them. Language, codec, channel layout, and track title make a more useful comparison.

    A title such as “English commentary” is descriptive metadata. The actual language tag and any selection flags are separate fields. Checking all three helps explain why a player offers or chooses a particular track.
