    Containers and Codecs

    Two files ending in .mp4 can have very different playback requirements. One might contain H.264 video and AAC audio; another might contain HEVC video and a different audio format. MP4 identifies the container, while H.264, HEVC, and AAC identify codecs.

    The distinction explains why changing a file extension cannot make a movie compatible. The player still has to open the package and decode the streams inside it.

    Layer Its job Example
    Container Stores tracks, timing, and descriptions MP4 or Matroska
    Video codec Represents the moving picture H.264 or HEVC
    Audio codec Represents the soundtrack AAC or FLAC

    These examples explain the layers; they are not a device support list.

    Repackaging versus re-encoding

    A remux copies encoded streams into a new container. The packaging changes, but the picture and sound need not be decoded or compressed again. A tool may adjust headers and metadata to meet the new container's requirements.

    Transcoding decodes and re-encodes one or more streams. That can change codec, bitrate, resolution, or other properties and requires more processing.

    In Emby's playback methods, a container mismatch can sometimes be handled through Direct Stream. A video codec mismatch may require video transcoding. Audio can be converted while the video is copied, so the processing cost is not always all-or-nothing.

    To understand a specific file, inspect both its container/codec combination and the streams selected for playback. Knowing only the container leaves the most important compatibility questions unanswered.
