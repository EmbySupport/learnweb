    Direct Stream Digital (DSD)

    DSD represents sound as a very fast stream of one-bit values. It is associated with Super Audio CD and downloadable music, and uses a different sample representation from ordinary multibit linear PCM.

    Its large sample-rate number needs context. DSD64 uses 2,822,400 one-bit samples per second per channel: 64 times 44.1 kHz. That is not equivalent to PCM at the same numerical rate and a 16- or 24-bit word length.

    Common label One-bit sample rate per channel
    DSD64 2.8224 MHz
    DSD128 5.6448 MHz
    DSD256 11.2896 MHz

    These labels describe the usual 44.1 kHz family of rates. DSD encoding uses noise shaping to keep much of its quantization noise outside the audible band; playback and conversion require appropriate filtering. A higher DSD rate alone cannot establish that one release sounds better than another master.

    Representation, file, and output

    DSD is not a container. DSF and DSDIFF are file formats that can store it. WavPack can compress DSD losslessly, preserving the one-bit data for later restoration.

    A player may send DSD through a supported DSD output path or convert it to PCM. Conversion to PCM involves filtering and a change of representation; it is not the same operation as losslessly unpacking a compressed DSD file.

    For DSD music in an Emby library, distinguish the stored stream from the stream delivered to the client and the signal received by the DAC. The source's DSD label alone does not confirm native DSD output. Its unusual rate and bit depth are useful identifiers, not a direct quality score against PCM.
