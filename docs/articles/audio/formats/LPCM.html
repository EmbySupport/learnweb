    Linear PCM (LPCM)

    Linear PCM, or LPCM, is audio represented directly by sample values. “Linear” means that equal numerical steps correspond to equal amplitude steps, unlike the companded PCM used in some telephone systems.

    LPCM is the familiar basis of digital music and decoded audio output. A receiver showing “PCM” is describing the signal it receives; the original file might have contained a compressed soundtrack.

    What its data rate means

    For tightly packed integer LPCM, the audio data rate is sample rate × bit depth × channel count.

    Example Calculation Audio payload rate
    Stereo, 44.1 kHz, 16-bit 44,100 × 16 × 2 1.4112 Mbit/s
    5.1, 48 kHz, 24-bit 48,000 × 24 × 6 6.912 Mbit/s
    7.1, 96 kHz, 24-bit 96,000 × 24 × 8 18.432 Mbit/s

    These figures exclude container and transport overhead. Storage can also use a wider word than the valid sample precision: 24 valid bits in a 32-bit slot occupy more space than tightly packed 24-bit samples.

    The outer format still matters

    WAV, AIFF, and other containers can store LPCM with different byte order and channel descriptions. “PCM” alone does not specify those details or a particular sample rate.

    Decoding a FLAC file restores PCM values. Decoding a lossy track also produces PCM, but those values are the codec's reconstruction rather than an exact copy of the pre-encoding source. The output label cannot distinguish the two.

    In an Emby playback path, PCM output can therefore be the expected result of decoding a compatible soundtrack. Whether every original channel reaches the speakers depends on the player's output format and the connected system.
