    Dolby TrueHD Audio

    Dolby TrueHD preserves the encoded audio without lossy compression. It is found in Blu-ray and Ultra HD Blu-ray movie soundtracks and may also carry Dolby Atmos information.

    Inspection tools can identify the stream using an MLP-related label, reflecting its Meridian Lossless Packing heritage. That does not make every MLP-based format interchangeable with TrueHD.

    Reading the format limits

    Blu-ray capability described by Dolby Supported configuration
    Up to eight full-range channels 96 kHz, 24-bit audio
    Up to six full-range channels 192 kHz, 24-bit audio
    Maximum encoded data rate Up to 18 Mbit/s

    These are capabilities, not the properties of every soundtrack. An actual track can use fewer channels, a lower sample rate, and a much lower average bitrate. Lossless compression also allows the encoded rate to vary with the content.

    Where decoding happens

    A capable player can decode TrueHD and send the resulting PCM channels to the audio system. If that output preserves the relevant sample values and channel presentation, local decoding does not itself introduce lossy compression.

    Alternatively, a supported bitstream output path lets a downstream device perform the decoding. TrueHD transport over HDMI uses Dolby MAT packaging; the details of that transport should not be confused with the codec stored in the file.

    For a TrueHD track with Atmos, ordinary channel PCM and metadata-bearing Atmos output are different results. Preserving lossless channel audio does not alone prove that the immersive information survived.

    An Emby movie may offer a separate AC-3 fallback, but TrueHD does not contain the same kind of embedded lossy core used by common DTS-HD disc streams. Confirm the available tracks rather than expecting an AC-3 mix to be extractable from any TrueHD file.
