    Dolby TrueHD Audio

    A receiver changes from “TrueHD” to “PCM” when a player decodes the soundtrack locally. The lossless representation has not necessarily been lost: TrueHD decoding restores its encoded audio presentation, and PCM is the form of those reconstructed samples.

    The important questions are what was decoded and what processing happened afterward.

    A lossless description can have a changing bitrate

    TrueHD uses Meridian Lossless Packing-related technology to describe audio reversibly. Like other lossless coding, it can exploit predictable signal relationships and retain the exact information needed to reconstruct the encoded presentation.

    Simple passages can require fewer bits than difficult ones. The source's sample rate and precision remain fixed while its compressed data rate can vary.

    For example, eight channels of 48 kHz, 24-bit PCM require 48,000 × 24 × 8 = 9.216 Mbit/s before compression. TrueHD's encoded rate depends on how efficiently that particular audio can be described; it is not the PCM calculation repeated with a fixed reduction ratio.

    Blu-ray capability described by Dolby Context
    Up to eight full-range channels at 96 kHz/24-bit A supported source configuration
    Up to six full-range channels at 192 kHz/24-bit Another supported source configuration
    Encoded data rates up to 18 Mbit/s A format/application capacity, not every track's average

    MLP-related labels in an inspection report reflect the coding heritage. They do not mean every earlier MLP-based delivery format is interchangeable with TrueHD.

    Compare two decode placements

    Receiver decode: a supported path carries the encoded TrueHD presentation onward, using suitable HDMI/MAT transport, and the receiver reconstructs it.

    Player decode: the player reconstructs the supported presentation and outputs PCM. If it keeps the intended sample values and channel roles, moving decoding into the player does not add lossy encoding.

    Subsequent downmixing, resampling, gain changes, or another codec conversion are additional operations. They should be recorded separately from the successful lossless decode.

    Atmos adds a spatial-information boundary

    A TrueHD track can carry Atmos information. Ordinary channel PCM supplies a fixed channel presentation; an object-aware path can carry the required spatial information onward, including supported MAT output.

    Lossless channel reconstruction and retaining a scene for later immersive rendering are thus different preservation questions. A player-rendered spatial output can still be useful even after the original metadata is no longer carried to the receiver.

    Where a conventional fallback comes from

    A disc arrangement may provide companion AC-3 audio for compatibility. TrueHD does not contain the DTS-HD-style embedded lossy core that can be extracted from common core-plus-extension soundtracks.

    For an Emby item with a TrueHD and separate AC-3 track, selecting the latter chooses another encoded presentation. If the server decodes TrueHD and encodes a compatible codec instead, that creates a new presentation. Comparing the selected track and route resolves which of these occurred.
