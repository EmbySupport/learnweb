    DTS-HD Master Audio

    DTS-HD Master Audio is a lossless audio format. In its common disc arrangement, a compatible lossy DTS core is combined with extension information that allows a full decoder to reconstruct the source audio exactly.

    The core's lossy nature does not make the complete Master Audio result lossy. The additional information corrects what the lossy representation alone cannot preserve.

    Data used by the decoder Result
    DTS core alone A compatible lossy presentation
    Core plus the required lossless extension The full lossless presentation
    A supported extension-only lossless stream Lossless decoding without a legacy core fallback

    The last arrangement matters: the DTS specification permits lossless streams without a backward-compatible lossy component. “DTS-HD” should not be treated as proof that an extractable core exists.

    Interpreting playback information

    A player that decodes the complete Master Audio presentation can output PCM. The receiver then identifies PCM rather than Master Audio because it receives decoded samples. That label does not imply an extra lossy conversion.

    A receiver showing ordinary DTS can indicate core-only delivery, but check its actual input information and the player's output before drawing that conclusion. A listening-mode name may describe processing applied after input detection.

    In Emby, the selected source track, server delivery, player decoding, and output transport form separate steps. A compatible video stream can reach the screen while the audio takes a fallback route.

    DTS:X can add immersive information in a compatible DTS-HD delivery form. Lossless reconstruction of a channel presentation and preservation of the immersive metadata are related but separate capabilities. DTS-HD High Resolution Audio is another distinct format and should not be substituted for Master Audio merely because both share “DTS-HD.”
