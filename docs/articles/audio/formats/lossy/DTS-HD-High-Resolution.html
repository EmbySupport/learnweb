    DTS-HD High Resolution Audio

    DTS-HD High Resolution Audio is lossy. Its name refers to an enhanced audio presentation, not to exact preservation of every original PCM sample. It is distinct from the lossless DTS-HD Master Audio format.

    In disc soundtracks, the High Resolution format can extend the conventional DTS core with additional audio information. Consumer presentations support up to 7.1 channels and 96 kHz sampling, according to the format description in Denon's receiver documentation.

    Playback result What has happened
    DTS-HD High Resolution decoding The supported enhanced presentation is reconstructed
    DTS core decoding Only the compatible lossy core presentation is used
    PCM received by the amplifier Decoding happened earlier in the path

    A PCM input label does not tell you which of the first two presentations was decoded. Channel count and the player's reported output can help distinguish them.

    Two easily confused quality labels

    A 24-bit or 96 kHz description concerns the audio sample representation supported by the format. It does not make lossy compression reversible. Likewise, a receiver that abbreviates its input as “DTS-HD” may not distinguish High Resolution from Master Audio on that particular display.

    An Emby movie can retain its High Resolution soundtrack while the player chooses a supported decoding or output route. The receiver's processing mode is another separate choice: upmixing a conventional input cannot recreate extension information that never reached it.

    When a setup plays DTS but not this DTS-HD variant, inspect the full source profile, available fallback component, and output path. Merely enabling a generic DTS option does not establish that every component understands the High Resolution extensions.
