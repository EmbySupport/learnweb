    DTS-HD High Resolution Audio

    DTS-HD High Resolution Audio is a lossy DTS-HD format that extends the conventional DTS presentation.

    DTS-HD High Resolution Audio is the lossy enhanced DTS-HD presentation. It is different from lossless DTS-HD Master Audio. Consumer descriptions include up to 7.1 channels and 96 kHz capability, providing more presentation scope than conventional core-only playback.

    The important word “lossy” remains compatible with improvements over a core.

    Additional information can reduce an error without eliminating it

    The DTS extension family includes XBR, which can encode quantized residual information describing what a core representation did not retain. Its decoder adds the reconstructed residual to the corresponding core representation.

    A numerical illustration makes the distinction clear:

    Step Illustrative value
    Source component +0.83
    Core reconstruction +0.75
    Difference to be corrected +0.08
    Quantized extension correction +0.05
    Enhanced reconstruction +0.80

    The enhancement moved the result closer to +0.83 but did not restore it exactly. These values illustrate quantized correction; they are not a DTS packet example or a complete specification of every High Resolution stream's extension combination.

    Lossless correction must preserve the necessary difference exactly. That is a different reconstruction promise, developed in DTS-HD Master Audio.

    Core-only decoding and the enhanced presentation

    A compatible core-only route uses the existing lossy core and misses the supported enhanced result. A full High Resolution decoder uses the appropriate extensions. Either decoder can then output ordinary PCM.

    A receiver's PCM label consequently identifies where decoding finished, not which DTS presentation was reconstructed. To distinguish the routes, identify the player's selected decoder/result and output layout.

    High-rate or 24-bit descriptions concern supported sample representation. They do not make the quantized correction in a lossy coding process reversible. The useful distinction is the reconstructed presentation and its codec profile, rather than treating every “DTS-HD” display abbreviation as Master Audio.
