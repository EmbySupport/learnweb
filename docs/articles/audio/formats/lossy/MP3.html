    MP3 Audio

    MP3 means MPEG Audio Layer III. It is a lossy audio format used for music, podcasts, and many older digital collections. The “3” is an audio layer number, not a reference to MPEG-3 video.

    An MP3 encoder reduces the data needed to describe sound, including by allocating precision according to what is likely to be audible. Decoding produces PCM, but not an exact restoration of the original recording's sample values.

    Reading an MP3 description

    Field Useful interpretation
    128 or 320 kbit/s Encoded data rate, not sample precision
    CBR A selected bitrate used throughout the stream
    VBR Bitrate changes with the encoder's allocation decisions
    Joint stereo Stereo coding can share information between channels
    44.1 kHz Audio sample rate, independent of encoded bitrate

    Joint stereo does not mean mono. For example, a mid/side representation describes what the channels share and how they differ, allowing the decoder to reconstruct left and right.

    At an average 192 kbit/s, four minutes of audio occupy about 5.76 MB before tags and other overhead. This is a storage example, not a universal quality recommendation.

    Audio and the file around it

    MP3 files usually store MPEG audio frames together with tags and, sometimes, information that helps seeking or accurate trimming. Album artwork and track titles do not belong to the compression mechanism itself.

    When playing an MP3 collection in Emby, separate the stored codec from any delivery conversion. Re-encoding an MP3 at a higher bitrate cannot recover details removed earlier. If another format is needed, retaining the original files avoids making the converted copy the only surviving version.
