    HE-AAC Audio

    HE-AAC stands for High Efficiency AAC. Its defining addition is Spectral Band Replication, or SBR, which helps retain a wide audio bandwidth while spending relatively few bits.

    The AAC-LC core encodes the lower part of the spectrum. SBR carries information that guides reconstruction of the upper part, using the decoded lower-frequency content as a starting point. It does not simply encode every high-frequency component in the same way as the core.

    Part What the decoder uses it for
    AAC-LC core Reconstructing the directly coded lower-frequency audio
    SBR information Reconstructing and shaping the upper-frequency audio
    Full HE-AAC decoder Combining both into the intended output

    This is a lossy process. The reconstructed upper band can sound useful and natural without being an exact copy of the original samples.

    Why two sample-rate numbers can appear

    An HE-AAC report may distinguish the core sample rate from the output rate after SBR processing. In a common dual-rate arrangement, a 24 kHz core produces 48 kHz output. That is a description of the coding structure, not proof that the source was captured at either rate.

    HE-AAC is used in broadcast and streaming systems where bandwidth matters. An Emby library can therefore encounter it in recorded programmes as well as in standalone audio.

    A decoder that handles only the AAC-LC core may reproduce a reduced-bandwidth version where the stream's signaling and packaging permit that fallback. It cannot reproduce the intended HE-AAC result without SBR support. HE-AAC v2 adds a further stereo coding tool, so identifying the full profile is more useful than treating all AAC labels as interchangeable.
