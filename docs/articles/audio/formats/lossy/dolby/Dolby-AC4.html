    Dolby AC-4 Audio

    AC-4 is a separate Dolby audio format, used in newer broadcast and delivery systems. It is not an AC-3 or E-AC-3 track with a new filename.

    The format can describe several ways to present the same programme. A presentation selects and combines the necessary audio components, allowing language, accessibility, or immersive choices within the coded structure.

    AC-4 concept Practical meaning
    Audio component A coded part of the programme
    Presentation A selected combination of components and rendering information
    Channel-based audio A defined speaker-channel arrangement
    Object-based audio Sound and associated spatial information for rendering

    Not every AC-4 programme uses every feature. Dialogue enhancement or audio description depends on what the content provides and what the playback system exposes.

    Why the recording can be harder to interpret

    A conventional list of tracks may not capture every selectable presentation inside one AC-4 bitstream. Seeing one audio stream does not necessarily mean that only one language or listening arrangement is available.

    AC-4 is one of the audio systems specified for ATSC 3.0 broadcasts. A recording can therefore contain usable video while its audio requires a different decoder from older TV recordings. Changing the container or relabeling the audio cannot supply that decoder.

    When an Emby library encounters such a recording, identify the actual audio format and presentation before investigating the delivery path. Support for Dolby Digital Plus or Dolby Atmos in another delivery form does not establish AC-4 support.

    If conversion is available, the resulting ordinary channel track represents a chosen presentation. It should not be assumed to retain all of the original bitstream's alternate languages, accessibility choices, or object information.
