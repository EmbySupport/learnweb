    Dolby Digital Plus (E-AC-3)

    Dolby Digital Plus is the Enhanced AC-3 lossy audio format used for multichannel and compatible immersive delivery.

    A Dolby Digital Plus programme can provide a conventional channel mix and a richer presentation using related substreams. Enhanced AC-3 (E-AC-3) extends AC-3's coding and presentation structure, while remaining a lossy audio format.

    Names such as E-AC-3, EC-3, and Dolby Digital Plus identify that encoded family.

    Build a presentation from substreams

    An independent substream supplies a decodable presentation without requiring its dependent substreams. A dependent substream is used with an associated independent one and can contribute additional or replacement channel information.

    This allows a compatible smaller channel presentation to coexist with information for a larger one. For example, a 7.1 arrangement can use a 5.1 independent presentation whose surround signals are partial combinations, plus dependent information that reconstructs the separate side/rear arrangement.

    The dependent information is not necessarily just “two more tracks.” The full decoder follows the specified channel mapping and replacement relationships to produce the intended presentation.

    Decoder uses Available result
    Independent E-AC-3 presentation Its compatible channel mix
    Independent plus required dependent information The complete associated channel presentation
    Supported object-carriage information too An immersive rendering input where supplied

    The exact structure belongs to the particular track. Consumer delivery commonly uses stereo, 5.1, and 7.1 presentations.

    Independence does not mean an AC-3 core

    An independent E-AC-3 substream is independent of other E-AC-3 substreams. It is not thereby a bitstream an AC-3-only decoder can reconstruct.

    Some disc arrangements include a compatible AC-3 component. A playback system can also produce AC-3 through a supported conversion. Those routes should be distinguished from extracting a pre-existing core from an arbitrary E-AC-3 file.

    This distinction explains why “backward compatible” needs its system context: compatibility can be supplied by packaging or an active conversion, rather than by the older decoder understanding the newer stream directly.

    Add Atmos as another reconstruction job

    Dolby Digital Plus can carry Atmos through backwards-compatible object audio carriage. The conventional channel presentation remains useful, while extra information supports recovery and rendering of the immersive representation.

    A decoder using only the compatible channel path produces ordinary channel audio. An object-aware route uses the spatial information too. The Atmos article follows that difference to a listening system.

    For an Emby source, a meaningful trace identifies which E-AC-3 presentation was delivered, whether it remained encoded or was decoded/converted, and what reached the renderer. This locates a specific substream or spatial-information boundary rather than treating a successful 5.1 output as proof of the whole source's feature set.
