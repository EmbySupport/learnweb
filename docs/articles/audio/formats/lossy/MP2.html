    MPEG Audio Layer II (MP2)

    MP2 is MPEG Audio Layer II, an audio codec often encountered in broadcast recordings. It is a different layer from MP3; its name does not mean “any audio accompanying MPEG-2 video.”

    Layer II divides the signal into frequency subbands and allocates bits among them. Its lossy coding preserves an approximation of the source rather than every original sample.

    Why it turns up in a library

    Encounter What MP2 tells you
    An older television recording The soundtrack may be Layer II even when the video is MPEG-2
    Conventional DAB radio Layer II is part of the original DAB audio system
    DAB+ radio The audio system uses the AAC family instead
    An MP2 elementary stream It contains encoded audio, not the whole TV programme

    Broadcast recordings commonly package the soundtrack inside a transport stream with video and timing. A file extension such as TS does not identify the audio codec by itself.

    Two-channel Layer II audio can be stereo or dual-channel material, depending on its coding mode. A channel count alone therefore does not tell you whether those channels should be heard together as a stereo pair.

    Compatibility after recording

    Keeping a recording's video intact does not require keeping an unsupported soundtrack unchanged. In an Emby Direct Stream session, compatible video can be copied while audio is converted for the client.

    Conversion may make an MP2 recording easier to play on a particular device, but another lossy encoding cannot improve on the retained source. For diagnosis, inspect the actual track's codec and channel information; changing the extension or assuming that the video codec dictates the audio does not resolve a decoding mismatch.
