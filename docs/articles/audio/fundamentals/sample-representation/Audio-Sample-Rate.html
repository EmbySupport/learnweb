    Audio Sample Rate

    Sample rate describes the spacing of audio samples in time. A 48 kHz track has 48,000 sample positions per second in every channel. It does not mean that the track contains a 48 kHz sound.

    For ordinary audio sampled around zero frequency, the highest representable frequency lies below half the sample rate. This boundary is the Nyquist frequency. Practical filters need some room before it, so the boundary is not a promise that every frequency up to that point is preserved equally well.

    Sample rate Nyquist frequency Time between samples
    44.1 kHz 22.05 kHz 22.68 microseconds
    48 kHz 24 kHz 20.83 microseconds
    96 kHz 48 kHz 10.42 microseconds

    What goes wrong above the limit

    Calculated demonstration: at a deliberately low 1 kHz sample rate, a 700 Hz sine and an inverted 300 Hz sine have identical sample values. This illustrates ambiguity after aliasing; it is not a recommended recording setup.

    Once an out-of-band signal has aliased into the recording, increasing the rate afterward cannot identify and restore the original frequency. Resampling changes the time grid of the available signal.

    The file's rate and the output device's rate can differ. A player or the operating system may convert 44.1 kHz material to a 48 kHz output while keeping the same pitch and duration. Merely relabeling the samples would change both.

    In Emby media information, sample rate is useful for understanding compatibility and the source format. A higher number alone does not prove a better recording, and it does not describe compression quality or the number of channels.
