    Dolby Atmos

    Dolby Atmos carries spatial information beyond a conventional fixed surround mix. A renderer uses that information to place sounds around and above the listener, according to the supported speaker or headphone arrangement.

    Atmos is not one fixed channel count or one storage bitrate. A basic media report can show 5.1 or 7.1 for a track whose encoded data also contains Atmos information. That number alone cannot confirm or rule out immersive rendering.

    The delivery form matters

    Common form Role in the playback path
    Dolby Digital Plus with Atmos Compressed delivery using audio and additional object information; lossy
    Dolby TrueHD with Atmos Lossless delivery of the encoded audio presentation with Atmos information
    Dolby MAT with Atmos Metadata-bearing transport used to carry decoded audio and Atmos information to a compatible system

    Dolby Digital Plus object carriage is specified in ETSI TS 103 420. It uses a compatible channel mix together with information for recovering and rendering objects. A decoder that supports the conventional presentation can play that presentation without reproducing the full Atmos experience.

    Dolby MAT is especially relevant to interpreting a receiver's input. Decoding an Atmos track does not necessarily destroy its spatial information: an object-aware path can carry the decoded audio and metadata onward. Ordinary channel PCM without that metadata is a different result.

    Source format and final rendering are separate

    A speaker description such as 5.1.4 describes the listening arrangement, not the number of objects in the file. The renderer distributes the spatial presentation over its supported outputs. A headphone render uses ear cues instead of separate physical height speakers.

    For Emby playback, check the selected track, the server's delivery method, and what the player sends to the audio system. Support for one Atmos delivery form does not establish support for every other one. A receiver can also apply an immersive listening mode to ordinary audio, so its processing-mode name and its detected input format answer different questions.
