    Virtual Surround

    Virtual surround uses signal processing to suggest sound positions beyond the available physical outputs. A headset marked “7.1,” for example, usually still has one main driver for each ear. The additional positions are represented by processed signals, not seven separate speakers around the listener.

    A headphone renderer can take a multichannel mix and filter each position into a binaural presentation. A speaker-based virtualizer works through a different acoustic arrangement, and can depend strongly on seating and the room.

    Input presentation What a virtualizer can use
    Discrete multichannel audio The existing channel positions
    Object-based audio with supported metadata The spatial descriptions provided by the source
    Ordinary stereo The information in two channels, possibly with derived spatial processing

    Virtualizing known surround positions and deriving new positions from stereo are different operations. A processor can make stereo feel wider or more enveloping without recovering separate surround recordings that never existed in the source.

    Convincing space depends on the listener

    Headphone spatialization uses timing and spectral cues associated with a head and ears. The model's fit, the headphones, and any head-tracking behavior affect the impression. “Virtual” describes the method, not a guarantee that every listener hears the same convincing external soundstage.

    For Emby playback, the processing may live in the operating system, headset software, television, soundbar, or receiver. The source track and the server's delivery method do not identify which of those effects is active.

    A stored binaural track already has ear-specific cues. Passing it through another spatializer may alter them. Likewise, a stereo-only path can leave a virtualizer with fewer source positions than a preserved multichannel path. To compare listening modes meaningfully, use the same track and level, and distinguish the detected input format from the system's selected effect.
