    7.1 Surround Sound

    In the common home-theater layout, 7.1 adds a rear surround pair to the five main-channel roles familiar from 5.1. Separate side and rear channels give a mix more control over sounds around and behind the listener.

    Schematic top view: three front channels, two side surrounds, and two rear surrounds. The separate LFE signal is shown without fixing a subwoofer position. This illustrates the common layout, not every possible eight-channel arrangement.

    Main-channel group Number of channels
    Front left, center, and front right 3
    Left and right side surround 2
    Left and right rear surround 2

    With LFE included, the track has eight channels. Those extra surrounds are still around the listener's plane; 7.1 alone does not describe height channels. A speaker arrangement such as 7.1.4 explicitly adds four height positions, and belongs to a different description of the playback system.

    Extra positions need the complete path

    A 7.1 source does not require every listener to own eight speakers. It can be reduced for another layout. The intended conversion combines the additional positions; dropping channels arbitrarily can lose sounds.

    Likewise, playing a 5.1 soundtrack through a receiver with seven main speakers does not give the file new discrete rear channels. The receiver may derive outputs through spatial processing, but that is a different source of information.

    For an Emby session, the channel label identifies what the selected track contains. The codec, client, and output connection determine how that presentation reaches the audio system. If eight channels appear in a report, check the actual layout and order, because channel count alone cannot distinguish rear surrounds from other possible positions.
