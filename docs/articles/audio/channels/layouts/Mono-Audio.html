    Mono Audio

    Mono audio uses one channel for the complete presentation. It can contain dialogue, music, and effects together, but it does not assign separate left and right positions to them.

    That one signal can be reproduced by one speaker, both stereo speakers, or an appropriate route in a larger system. Hearing it from two speakers does not turn the source into stereo.

    Stored presentation What the channels contain
    One-channel mono One complete signal
    Two channels with identical audio The same mono presentation in both channels
    Two different independent mono programmes Two selections or signals; not necessarily a stereo mix

    Older television material, archival recordings, and some spoken-word content naturally use mono. Its usefulness does not depend on having fewer musical instruments or on using a low bitrate: a complex mix can still be mono.

    Mono and a center channel are different

    A mono track is the entire soundtrack. The center channel is one part of a multichannel soundtrack, commonly used for screen-centered dialogue and other front sounds. Selecting a mono track should not mean discarding all the other parts of a surround mix.

    Conversely, converting a stereo mix to mono requires combining left and right. Opposing signal components can partially cancel when summed, so a stereo mix with an unusual phase relationship may change noticeably in mono. Merely taking one side can lose material that exists on the other side.

    In Emby, a mono label helps identify the track's layout. It does not indicate an incomplete delivery or require audio conversion by itself. If an old programme sounds equally strong from both sides of a stereo system, that can be exactly the intended playback of its mono source.
