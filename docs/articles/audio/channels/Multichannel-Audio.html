    Multichannel Audio

    Multichannel audio carries several sound channels within one audio presentation. A film's left, center, right, and surround signals can therefore remain separate until the playback system decides how to reproduce them.

    Channels are not the same as selectable tracks. A single 5.1 English track normally contains six channels. A second 5.1 French track contains another complete presentation; it is not one of the first track's six channels.

    Information What it answers
    Channel count How many signals the presentation carries
    Channel layout Which roles those signals have
    Channel order Where each role appears in the data
    Output arrangement Which speakers or headphone rendering will reproduce it

    The layout matters as much as the count. Four signals intended for front and rear positions are different from four unrelated recording stems. Software needs their meaning, not just the number four.

    A source layout is not a speaker inventory

    A six-channel soundtrack can be played on a stereo television by combining its channels appropriately. A receiver can also redirect bass from main channels to a subwoofer. Neither operation changes how many channels the original soundtrack contains.

    When Emby reports a source as 5.1, that identifies the track, not a guarantee of six independent output paths all the way to the room. The client, connection, and audio system must support the presentation, or a conversion is needed.

    Likewise, object-based audio can carry spatial instructions beyond the channels visible in a basic media report. A plain channel count cannot establish whether those instructions survive playback.

    If dialogue comes from an unexpected speaker or effects appear in front instead of behind, check channel mapping. More channels cannot compensate for channels sent to the wrong places.
