    Overview: Audio devices and operating-system output

    Audio devices and operating-system interfaces connect a player's output to its physical or wireless listening path.

    Decoded samples still have to pass through a device interface, an operating-system audio path and, eventually, a signal converter or receiver. S/PDIF, USB and Bluetooth introduce different transport arrangements. The operating-system and exclusive-mode topics explain how software reaches those arrangements and where format or mixing decisions can occur.

    • Digital-to-analog conversion (DAC)
      A DAC converts timed digital samples into an analog signal using conversion circuitry, reconstruction filtering, and an output stage.

    • S/PDIF
      S/PDIF transports a consumer digital audio stream whose framing can carry stereo PCM or supported encoded audio bursts.

    • USB Audio
      USB Audio defines discoverable audio streams and controls, with packet delivery synchronized to the sample clock used by the device.

    • Bluetooth connections
      Bluetooth audio uses particular profiles and transport arrangements to connect an audio source with a wireless listening device.

    • Operating-system audio APIs
      Operating-system audio APIs define how applications exchange audio with devices and system processing.

    • Exclusive-mode audio output
      Exclusive audio output grants an application a dedicated endpoint route that bypasses a shared mixer while retaining the device's format and timing requirements.
