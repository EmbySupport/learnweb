    Audio Transcoding

    Audio transcoding makes a new encoded audio stream from a decoded source. It can change the codec, bitrate, channel layout, or sample rate to suit the playback device and delivery conditions.

    In Emby, incompatible audio does not necessarily require re-encoding compatible video. A Direct Stream session can copy video while converting the soundtrack.

    Audio operation What happens to the samples
    Compatible lossless-to-lossless conversion Can preserve them when the formats and processing allow it
    Lossless to lossy Introduces the destination codec's approximation
    Lossy to lossy Encodes an already reconstructed signal again
    Downmixing or resampling during conversion Changes the presentation even before the final encoder

    By contrast, copying encoded packets into different packaging is remuxing. It does not make a new audio encoding.

    Why the target bitrate is not a restoration tool

    Giving an encoder a larger data budget can reduce new compression damage. It cannot restore detail discarded by an earlier lossy codec. A high-bitrate AAC delivery made from a low-bitrate MP3 remains limited by that input.

    Transcoding can also change more than fidelity. A multichannel track may become stereo, and an ordinary channel encoder may omit the object information of an immersive source. Producing another E-AC-3 stream, for example, does not automatically recreate an original Atmos presentation.

    For diagnosis, compare the source track with the delivered audio: codec, channel layout, bitrate, and relevant spatial information. The server's activity label tells you that conversion occurred; those details explain what changed.

    A converted stream can be the appropriate compatible result for a client. Keeping the original library file preserves the option to use its original presentation on another supported playback path.
