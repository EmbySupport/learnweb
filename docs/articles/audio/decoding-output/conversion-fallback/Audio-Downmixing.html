    Audio Downmixing

    Downmixing combines audio channels for a smaller output layout, such as turning a 5.1 film soundtrack into stereo for a television or headphones.

    It should preserve useful content from the source presentation. Simply retaining front left and front right can omit much of the dialogue carried by the center channel.

    A stereo fold-down

    Source role Typical treatment in a stereo downmix
    Front left and right Contribute to the corresponding output side
    Center Contributes to both output sides
    Surrounds Contribute according to the selected mixing matrix
    LFE Treatment depends on the downmix design and playback system

    This is a routing overview, not a universal coefficient recipe. Ordinary stereo and matrix-compatible stereo can use different relationships and phase treatment.

    Combining channels also raises a numerical problem. If two channels each contain a simultaneous sample of +0.8, adding them at full gain produces +1.6, beyond the usual normalized full-scale limit. Mixing coefficients, gain adjustment, or other overload handling provide headroom. Channel reduction can change level without involving another lossy codec.

    Where the layout changed

    An Emby session may downmix during server conversion, client decoding, or output processing. If dialogue becomes weak, compare the source's center content with the resulting stereo channels and verify that the source layout was interpreted correctly.

    The LFE channel is not the same as all bass in the soundtrack. Bass management can redirect low frequencies from ordinary channels to a subwoofer separately from the LFE mix.

    A downmix preserves a useful smaller presentation, but it cannot keep every original channel independently available. Later upmixing cannot reverse that combination into the original discrete tracks.
