    Audio Bitstream Passthrough

    Bitstream passthrough leaves audio decoding to a downstream device, such as an AV receiver. The player sends an encoded presentation through a compatible output path instead of turning it into ordinary PCM channels first.

    That is separate from Emby's server-side Direct Play decision. A client can receive an unchanged file and still decode its soundtrack locally.

    Several capabilities have to agree

    Part of the route Required capability
    Player Recognize and output the specific encoded format
    Operating system and driver Expose a suitable encoded-audio output path
    Connection and intervening devices Carry that format through the selected route
    Receiver or soundbar Decode the delivered presentation

    Support at the final receiver does not remove a limitation earlier in the chain. A television forwarding audio can be a separate compatibility step even when it does not decode the soundtrack itself.

    IEC 61937 defines carriage of encoded audio over interfaces based on the digital audio framework. Different codec types have different transport requirements; the presence of an HDMI connector alone does not prove that a particular app can pass every format.

    Processing changes the question

    Mixing system sounds into a soundtrack or applying ordinary sample-based volume control generally requires decoded audio or a specially supported processing path. A passthrough option may therefore change which local controls are available.

    The receiver's detected input helps confirm what was delivered. Its chosen listening mode can add processing after decoding, so a surround-mode label is not proof of the original bitstream.

    For an Emby movie with immersive audio, passthrough can preserve suitable encoded spatial information when every stage supports it. An object-aware MAT output is another possible route; plain channel PCM and metadata-bearing transport should be distinguished.
