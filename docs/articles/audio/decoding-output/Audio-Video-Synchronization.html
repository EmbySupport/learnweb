    Audio/Video Synchronization

    Audio/video synchronization means presenting related sound and picture at the right time. A clap or a clearly spoken consonant makes an offset easy to notice because the visual event and its sound belong together.

    The streams have different units: video is organized into frames, while audio is organized into samples and coded audio frames. Timestamps relate those units to a playback timeline. Equal packet arrival times are not the goal.

    Constant offset or growing drift?

    Observation What it suggests
    The same mismatch throughout the programme A fixed timing offset or processing delay
    Alignment starts well, then gradually worsens A rate, clock, or timeline mismatch
    A sudden change after a seek or discontinuity Re-establishment of timing may be involved
    Only one audio output route is affected Delay in that route is worth checking

    These are diagnostic clues, not complete fault diagnoses.

    A fixed offset can often be addressed by delaying the earlier presentation. For example, if sound arrives 80 ms before its matching picture, adding 80 ms of audio delay aligns that particular route. Interfaces use different signs for their adjustment controls, so verify the result rather than assuming what “positive” means.

    A single offset cannot fix continuing drift. A hypothetical clock-rate difference of 50 parts per million accumulates 0.18 seconds in one hour: 3,600 × 50 / 1,000,000. Changing the starting delay leaves that rate difference intact.

    In an Emby session, compare the same passage through another client or output route and note whether the mismatch is constant or grows. Display processing, decoded-audio buffering, and wireless output can add different delays. Keeping those observations separate from the file's timestamps narrows where to investigate.
