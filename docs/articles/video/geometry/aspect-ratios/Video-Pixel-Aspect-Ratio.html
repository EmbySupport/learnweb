    Video Pixel Aspect Ratio

    A video sample does not always represent a square on the screen. Pixel aspect ratio (PAR) describes its intended width relative to its height. PAR 1:1 means square pixels; PAR 4:3 means that each sample is presented one-third wider than it is tall.

    FFmpeg calls this sample aspect ratio, often abbreviated SAR. Other contexts use SAR for the stored frame's width-to-height ratio, so check the tool's definition before comparing numbers.

    Original schematic. Both grids contain the same number of samples; their presentation widths differ.

    Pixel aspect ratio What it does to an unchanged grid
    1:1 Preserves the grid's numerical width-to-height ratio
    4:3 Makes the displayed picture one-third wider
    16:15 Makes the displayed picture about 6.7% wider

    Why the metadata matters

    A 1440 × 1080 frame has a stored ratio of 4:3. With PAR 4:3, its display ratio becomes 16:9. Treating the samples as square squeezes the picture horizontally, even though every sample is still present.

    That makes PAR particularly useful when investigating older television recordings and anamorphic sources in an Emby library. Different files with identical stored dimensions can legitimately have different display shapes.

    Changing a PAR flag tells the player to interpret the existing samples differently. It does not resample the image or create additional detail. Converting the picture to an equivalent square-pixel grid is a separate processing operation.

    Be cautious with standard-definition rules copied from a format name. Whether a workflow describes the whole coded raster or a smaller active region can affect the expected ratio. The encoded signaling, container display information, and a recognizable object in the picture provide better evidence than dimensions alone.
