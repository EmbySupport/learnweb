    Video Resolution

    Video resolution is the number of picture samples across and down a frame. A 1920 × 1080 video has 2,073,600 positions in its main picture grid: a little over two million.

    A resolution label describes that grid, rather than the amount of visible detail. A sharp 1080p source can look better than a poorly compressed 4K copy. Enlarging an older recording adds output pixels, but cannot recover information that was absent from the recording.

    Familiar label Common stored dimensions Main-grid samples per frame
    720p HD 1280 × 720 921,600
    1080p Full HD 1920 × 1080 2,073,600
    2160p Ultra HD 3840 × 2160 8,294,400
    DCI 4K full container 4096 × 2160 8,847,360

    These are common full-frame formats. A cropped film can have a different height, and “4K” alone does not identify one exact width.

    Count the picture, then interpret its shape

    The width-to-height ratio of the stored grid is not always the displayed shape. Anamorphic video uses nonsquare pixels, so a 1440 × 1080 grid can represent a 16:9 picture. Rotation metadata can also turn a landscape grid into a portrait presentation.

    Black bars introduce another distinction. A 1920 × 1080 file can include a film occupying only 1920 × 800 pixels, with the remaining rows storing the bars. The file is still 1920 × 1080; the active image is smaller.

    For Emby playback, more samples generally mean more scaling and decoding work. Ultra HD contains four times as many main-grid positions as Full HD. That does not guarantee a file four times as large: bitrate, frame rate, compression, and the scene itself also matter. Read the resolution alongside those properties when comparing versions of a film.
