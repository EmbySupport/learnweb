    Video Buffering Verifier (VBV)

    VBV, or Video Buffering Verifier, describes a modeled buffer used to constrain coded video. The encoder must produce pictures that can be supplied and removed under the model's rate, capacity, and timing rules.

    It addresses a problem that average bitrate cannot: a video may fit its whole-film data budget but contain an individual picture or short section too demanding for the intended decoder buffer.

    Conceptual decoder-buffer view. Actual conformance depends on codec-specific timing and picture removals; this drawing is not a bitstream verification result.

    Rate and capacity have different units

    Illustrative parameter Value Meaning
    Input-rate limit 4 Mb/s A data rate
    Buffer capacity 16 megabits, or 2 MB An amount of data
    Capacity divided by rate 4 seconds A useful time-equivalent scale

    Decimal units. The ratio is not a guaranteed playback startup delay or a measurement of an app's buffer.

    For a particular input rate, a larger modeled capacity gives more room for uneven picture sizes. A small capacity forces the encoder to control those demands more tightly. Initial occupancy and removal timing also matter.

    Codec standards use related models with their own terminology, such as the hypothetical reference decoder and coded picture buffer in H.264. Encoder options named “VBV” should be read with their implementation's documentation.

    In Emby, a converted stream's rate-control constraints help explain why quality changes during difficult scenes. A quality-based encode combined with a restrictive rate and buffer limit may need stronger compression locally.

    The modeled coding buffer is separate from the player's downloaded-data buffer and the network's actual behavior. Increasing a playback buffer cannot make an encoded stream satisfy a decoder limit it already exceeds.
