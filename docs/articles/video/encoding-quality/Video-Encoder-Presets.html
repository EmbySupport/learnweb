    Video Encoder Presets

    An encoder preset chooses a bundle of settings controlling how much work an encoder spends on its decisions. It can change searches for motion, picture partitioning, reference use, lookahead, and other analysis.

    A slower preset often gives the encoder more opportunities to use its bitrate efficiently. That can improve the picture at a fixed rate or reduce the data needed for a comparable picture. The result depends on the encoder and source; “slow” is not a fixed quality level.

    Choice What it controls
    Preset Encoding effort and tool choices
    Bitrate or quality target The rate-control objective
    Profile and level Bitstream compatibility constraints
    Tuning Priorities for a source type or use case

    These controls interact. Changing a preset while retaining the same CRF can change the size and picture, because the encoder makes different decisions. It is not safe to assume that the slower result must always be smaller.

    The labels are implementation-specific

    x265 names presets from ultrafast through placebo. A hardware encoder can use a different scale and different internal tools. A “medium” preset in two encoders does not establish equivalent speed, efficiency, or resource use.

    More encoding work also does not translate directly into more decoding work. The encoder's search can be expensive while producing a stream with similar decoder requirements. Check its actual profile and tool choices rather than judging playback compatibility from the preset name.

    For Emby video transcoding, the encoder has to supply pictures quickly enough for playback. Extra analysis is useful only within the available processing budget, including other sessions and required filters.

    For an offline library conversion, more time may be acceptable. Compare representative scenes and the resulting rate or size, and keep the source fixed. Moving from a fast to a slow preset cannot recover detail that the source's earlier compression has already removed.
