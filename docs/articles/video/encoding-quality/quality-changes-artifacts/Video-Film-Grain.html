    Film Grain in Compressed Video

    Film grain is fine texture associated with photographic film. A digital video may retain that texture from a scan or add a grain effect deliberately. It can be part of the intended appearance rather than a defect to remove.

    Grain is difficult to compress because it changes across pictures and is less predictable than stable scenery. An encoder may need substantial data to retain it, or may simplify it when the budget is tight.

    Treatment What to look for
    Encode the visible grain directly The texture must fit the picture's data budget
    Filter it before encoding Smoother pictures, with a risk of removing real detail
    Use a grain-aware encoder tuning Different decisions about preserving the texture
    Encode a cleaner picture with grain parameters The decoder generates modeled grain for display

    Grain can change without the resolution changing

    A converted film can retain the same width and height yet lose its fine texture. Grain may become softer, form larger patches, or pulse between pictures. Inspect moving scenes: a paused frame does not show how consistently the texture behaves.

    Some encoders offer a grain tuning. This changes their priorities rather than restoring grain lost before the encode. The setting is implementation-specific, and its effect on bitrate should be assessed using the actual source.

    AV1 can signal film-grain parameters so grain is synthesized as part of output processing. This aims to reproduce the appearance of grain without coding every original grain sample in the picture. It is not exact preservation of the source's grain pattern.

    For Emby, grain differences between direct playback and a converted stream can explain why the same film looks smoother even when the delivered resolution matches. Check the source, rate-control constraints, and any filtering in the conversion path.

    If the original appearance matters, retain the source while evaluating smaller derivatives. A later encoder cannot reconstruct the original pattern from a copy that has already removed it.
