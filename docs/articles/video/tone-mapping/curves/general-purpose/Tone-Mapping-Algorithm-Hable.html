    Tone Mapping Curve: Hable

    Hable is the tone mapping curve John Hable developed for Uncharted 2. Its aim was a film-like response: bright areas approach white gradually, while the darker parts of the image also receive deliberate shaping. Hable describes its origins and publishes the original formula in Filmic Tonemapping Operators.

    FFmpeg 8.1 Hable curve: source peak 8, output white 1, relative linear-light values for neutral gray. Dashed gray shows clipping. This illustrates the curve, not an Emby preset.

    The close-up shows that this version already changes lower values; it is not an unchanged section followed only by a highlight knee.

    That makes it more than a way to squeeze down the brightest pixels. The filmic shape has three parts:

    Part of the curve What it shapes
    Toe The transition out of black and the separation of dark tones
    Middle section The brightness and contrast of ordinary scene content
    Shoulder The gradual compression of strong highlights toward white

    The appeal during HDR-to-SDR conversion is that a bright reflection can retain gradations instead of becoming a flat white patch. The tradeoff is that midtones can become darker too. If an Emby playback comparison preserves the sky but makes a person's face noticeably dimmer, the tone curve is one possible contributor; the brightest highlights alone do not tell the whole story.

    The name does not specify every processing choice. For example, FFmpeg's software implementation uses fixed curve coefficients and normalizes the result against the source peak. Different peak assumptions can therefore change the result even with the same curve selected.

    Hable is useful to recognize in existing settings and discussions, but it is not a universal quality recommendation: current libplacebo documentation treats it as a legacy option.
