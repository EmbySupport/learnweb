    Groups of Pictures (GOPs)

    A group of pictures (GOP) describes a run of coded pictures and the relationships between them. A common structure combines intra-coded pictures with pictures predicted from one or more references.

    The familiar letters are I for intra-coded, P for predictive, and B for bi-predictive picture types. Their precise rules vary by codec. In particular, modern B-pictures can themselves be used as references; “B” does not always mean disposable.

    Original dependency example. B-pictures here are not references: B1/B2 use I0 and P3, and B4/B5 use P3 and P6. The shown decode order is one valid choice for this schematic, not a universal codec sequence.

    Length is a tradeoff

    At a constant 24 fps, 48 pictures span two seconds. If clean access points are placed every 48 pictures, that creates two-second spacing. Scene changes and encoder decisions can introduce shorter intervals.

    Structure choice Practical effect
    More frequent access points Less decoding from an earlier point may be needed after seeking
    Longer prediction runs Can improve compression, depending on content and encoder choices
    Closed GOP The group can be decoded without picture references from an earlier group
    Open GOP Prediction relationships can cross the nominal group boundary

    An open GOP is not inherently damaged. It needs correct access-point handling so the decoder knows which pictures are valid when starting partway through the stream.

    In an Emby session, GOP structure can influence seeking, the beginning of a transcode, and how quickly decoding recovers after damaged source data. It does not set the programme's frame rate, and a longer GOP does not necessarily require a longer startup delay in every playback route.

    File size and visible quality alone do not reveal this structure. Picture-level inspection is useful when diagnosing irregular access points or a file that seeks poorly despite playing normally from the beginning.
