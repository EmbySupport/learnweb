    Video Keyframes and Random Access

    When you seek into a film, the next picture you want may depend on earlier decoded pictures. A random-access point gives the decoder a suitable place to begin reconstructing the stream. Containers and tools commonly mark these entry points as keyframes.

    The player can start before your requested time, decode the necessary pictures, and suppress them until the requested picture is ready. That is why an exact seek and the physical start of decoding need not be the same position.

    Example with entry points every two seconds What decoding may need
    Seek to 10.0 seconds Start at the entry point at 10.0 seconds
    Seek to 11.3 seconds Start at 10.0 seconds and decode forward
    Seek to 12.0 seconds Start at the next entry point at 12.0 seconds

    Illustrative timeline. Real files can have irregular entry points, preroll rules, and container-specific seeking behaviour.

    An I-picture is only part of the story

    An intra-coded picture does not predict its own samples from other pictures. However, following pictures or pictures reordered around it may still depend on earlier references. A clean entry point must satisfy the codec's dependency rules, not merely have an “I” label.

    H.264's IDR pictures establish a reference reset. HEVC also defines several kinds of random-access pictures, with rules for pictures that lead the access point in display order. The decoder still needs suitable stream configuration.

    For Emby, the spacing and validity of these points can affect seeking responsiveness and the start of transcoded output. A longer gap can mean more source pictures must be decoded before reaching the chosen time. Network delivery, storage speed, and decoder performance also contribute.

    Changing a packet's keyframe flag cannot remove prediction dependencies. A damaged index or incorrectly marked entry point can therefore cause seeking failures even when uninterrupted playback seems fine.

    Groups of pictures describe the surrounding structure; shorter groups can provide more frequent entry points at a potential compression cost.
