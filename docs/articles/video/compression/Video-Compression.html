    How Video Compression Works

    Video compression describes pictures using fewer bits than a straightforward list of their sample values. It finds patterns within a picture and, in many codecs, similarities between successive pictures.

    Imagine a camera following a person across a plain wall. Much of the wall stays similar from one picture to the next. An encoder can describe a prediction of that region and a correction, rather than repeatedly describing it from scratch.

    Idea What it contributes
    Prediction Estimates samples from information the decoder already has
    Correction, or residual Describes the difference between the estimate and the picture
    Entropy coding Represents common values and patterns with compact codes
    Quantization in lossy modes Approximates values to reduce the amount of information retained

    This is a conceptual breakdown. Different codecs combine their tools differently; not every format uses every row.

    The decoder reconstructs complete pictures

    Prediction does not leave the viewer with only “changed pixels.” The decoder follows the coded instructions and reconstructs the complete pictures needed for display. The codec specification defines the rules; the encoder decides which allowed tools to use.

    A good encoder can spend more time evaluating possibilities to obtain a smaller stream at a similar visible quality. That is why two files with the same codec, dimensions, and frame rate can have different bitrates and quality.

    For Emby, compression connects storage, network capacity, and playback capabilities. A smaller video stream is easier to deliver over a constrained connection, but the app must support its codec and stream requirements. During video transcoding, the server decodes the original and compresses a new stream, adding work and potentially another lossy step.

    Lossless compression reproduces the input samples exactly. Lossy compression permits differences in exchange for greater savings. Neither term says whether the file's audio or other tracks use the same approach.
