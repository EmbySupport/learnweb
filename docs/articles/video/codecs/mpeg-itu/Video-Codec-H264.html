    H.264 / AVC Video

    H.264 and AVC are names for the same video coding standard. AVC means Advanced Video Coding; its corresponding MPEG specification is MPEG-4 Part 10. It is different from the older MPEG-4 Part 2 format.

    H.264 is familiar in disc-derived media, camera recordings, and streaming files. Its encoded pictures may live in different containers, so an MP4, MKV, or transport stream is not a separate kind of H.264.

    Label you may encounter What it identifies
    H.264 / AVC The coding standard and bitstream format
    x264 A software encoder that creates H.264
    Baseline, Main, High, High 10 Different H.264 profiles
    AVC1 or a similar container identifier How the container identifies its H.264 track

    An encoder chooses how to use the standard's tools. Different software or hardware encoders can therefore produce compatible H.264 streams with different bitrates, quality, and encoding effort. “Encoded with x264” does not require an x264 decoder.

    Which H.264 file?

    The profile matters. Ordinary 8-bit H.264 and High 10 H.264 place different requirements on a decoder. A device can provide hardware decoding for one while relying on software, or lacking an app playback path, for the other.

    The level adds limits such as picture size, processing rate, and buffering. Resolution alone leaves out frame rate and other resource demands.

    In Emby, H.264 can be the original library format or the output of video transcoding. An H.264 source still needs conversion if other stream characteristics or delivery requirements are incompatible. Compare the source and delivered stream information when troubleshooting: seeing “H.264” in both places does not mean the video was copied unchanged.
