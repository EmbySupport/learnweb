    H.265 / HEVC Video

    H.265 and HEVC identify the same coding standard. HEVC stands for High Efficiency Video Coding. It is frequently encountered in UHD films and recordings, including files carrying HDR video.

    The format offers more flexible ways to divide and predict pictures than H.264. The practical aim is to preserve useful picture quality with less data. There is no fixed file-size saving that applies to every source or encoder: texture, grain, settings, and the quality being compared all affect the result.

    Common HEVC profile Sample depth Color sampling
    Main 8-bit 4:2:0
    Main 10 8-bit or 10-bit 4:2:0

    Selected profiles only. Other HEVC profiles cover additional formats and tools.

    A Main 10 label does not establish that the pictures are HDR. HDR10 uses HEVC in common delivery workflows, but HDR also involves transfer characteristics, color information, metadata, and the display path. An SDR file can use 10-bit HEVC.

    Check decoding separately from encoding

    x265 is one software encoder that creates HEVC. It is not another name for the format, and a player does not need x265 to decode the result.

    For an Emby playback session, check the app's decoder, profile, level, resolution, and frame rate. The server's ability to decode a source for transcoding is a separate capability from the app's ability to play it directly. Hardware decoding and hardware encoding are separate capabilities too.

    A supported HEVC source can still be transcoded because of a bitrate limit, subtitle processing, or another playback requirement. Conversely, a smaller HEVC file is not necessarily easier for an older device to decode than a larger H.264 file.
