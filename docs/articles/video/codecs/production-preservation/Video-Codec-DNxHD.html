    Avid DNxHD Video

    Avid DNxHD is the familiar name for HD production workflows in the VC-3 video coding family. It encodes pictures independently, making it useful for editing, exchange, and retained production copies.

    Traditional DNxHD modes use defined HD raster and coding combinations. A label such as “DNxHD 145” belongs to a particular picture format and rate; the number should not be treated as a quality score that transfers unchanged to every frame rate.

    Detail to record Why it matters
    Raster and frame rate Select the applicable legacy coding mode
    Quality or bitrate label Distinguishes representations within that mode
    Bit depth and sample format Establish decoder and processing requirements
    Container Describes how the coded pictures and audio are packaged

    DNxHD is compressed and lossy. Its suitability for production does not mean the reconstructed pictures are mathematically identical to the input. Retaining a good master can limit further generation loss during later delivery conversions.

    The naming has changed

    Avid now uses Avid DNx as the unified name, dropping the HD, HR, and GX suffixes from its newer naming scheme. That does not change what an older file contains. Read historical DNxHD modes in their original context rather than applying every capability of the newer unified codec to them.

    DNxHR is the related name associated with resolution-independent production workflows. Neither suffix, nor the newer unified name, is enough to establish direct playback compatibility.

    For Emby, a DNxHD production file may need conversion into a format suitable for the app and available bandwidth. Preserve it as the source when creating that derivative. Encoding an already reduced consumer copy as DNxHD can make it easier to edit, but cannot restore lost texture or detail.
