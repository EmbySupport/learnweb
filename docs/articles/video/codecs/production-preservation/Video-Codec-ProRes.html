    Apple ProRes Video

    Apple ProRes is a family of codecs aimed at production and editing. Its ordinary video variants encode each picture independently, allowing editors to work without reconstructing a long chain of predicted pictures.

    That convenience and the retained picture information often come with a higher data rate than a delivery encode. A large ProRes export can therefore be useful as a production source while being inconvenient to stream to ordinary playback devices.

    ProRes group Main distinction
    422 variants: Proxy, LT, 422, HQ Different quality and data-rate targets for 4:2:2 video
    4444 and 4444 XQ Higher-fidelity color workflows and optional alpha
    ProRes RAW and RAW HQ A separate RAW sensor-data workflow

    The 422 and 4444 picture coding is lossy, even when its output is visually very close to the input. Alpha in the 4444 variants is encoded losslessly; that does not make their color-picture coding lossless. ProRes RAW should not be treated as another ordinary 422 playback profile.

    Production source and playback version

    A ProRes file's name does not establish its exact variant, color interpretation, or decoder requirements. Inspect the profile and stream details. A MOV extension only identifies packaging, and MOV can carry other codecs.

    For Emby, a ProRes master can be the input to a more suitable playback version when the server has the necessary decoder. Direct playback depends on the receiving app and its selected framework, rather than the codec's usefulness in an editing program.

    Transcoding a heavily compressed source into ProRes does not recreate camera detail. It can provide a convenient intermediate for further work, but the source's earlier losses remain. When possible, create delivery versions from the best retained master rather than from an already reduced streaming copy.
