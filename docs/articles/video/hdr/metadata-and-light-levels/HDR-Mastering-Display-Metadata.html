    Mastering Display Metadata: SMPTE ST 2086

    SMPTE ST 2086 records the color volume of the mastering display: the display configuration on which the finished picture was judged. It provides context for reproducing the picture elsewhere.

    Metadata component What it describes
    Red, green, and blue primaries The display's configured primary colors
    White point The chromaticity used for neutral white
    Maximum mastering luminance The top of the configured luminance range
    Minimum mastering luminance The bottom of that range

    Suppose a report lists a mastering maximum of 1,000 nits. That does not say that every frame, or even the brightest pixel, reaches 1,000 nits. The display could have been capable of showing a brighter highlight than the filmmaker chose to use. Content light levels are described separately by MaxCLL and MaxFALL.

    Another apparently contradictory combination is a BT.2020 video signal with P3 mastering primaries. The encoded signal and the mastering display are different things: the wider encoding space can carry colors graded on the narrower display. The two labels do not need to match.

    ST 2086 also does not specify the complete tone-mapping recipe. It does not contain shot-by-shot creative adjustments or define the mastering display's transfer function.

    In an Emby media report, read these fields as source context, not as minimum requirements for your television. A display with a different color volume needs adaptation, but that alone does not make the file unsuitable for playback. Tone mapping explains the brightness part of that adaptation.
