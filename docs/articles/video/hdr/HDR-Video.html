    What HDR Video Adds to SDR

    A lamp and the wall it illuminates do not look equally bright in real life. HDR video gives a picture more room to preserve that difference: the lamp can stand out while the wall remains at a comfortable level.

    The improvement is about the range within the image. An HDR film can still contain a dark room, a muted landscape, or an intentionally low-contrast shot. Making every part of the image brighter would change the creative decision rather than demonstrate HDR.

    Picture characteristic What it tells you
    Dynamic range How far the picture can extend between dark and bright levels
    Resolution How many picture samples describe the scene
    Color gamut Which colors can be represented
    Bit depth How finely the signal values are divided

    These characteristics can improve together, but they are separate. A 4K file is not necessarily HDR, and ten-bit encoding alone is not enough to identify HDR. BT.2100 defines HDR image parameters, including the PQ and HLG systems.

    Why two HDR screens can look different

    A file describes an image; it cannot guarantee that every display has enough brightness or color capability to reproduce it exactly. Tone mapping adapts the image to those limits. Room lighting and display settings also affect what you see.

    When comparing playback through Emby, use the same scene and note the playback method. The source's HDR label and the display's active output mode answer different questions. A file can begin as HDR and reach an SDR screen after conversion, or arrive unchanged and be adapted by the player.
