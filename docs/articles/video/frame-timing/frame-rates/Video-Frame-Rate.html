    Video Frame Rate

    Frame rate describes how many complete video pictures are presented each second, usually written as frames per second or fps. At 24 fps, successive pictures are normally about 41.7 milliseconds apart; at 60 fps, they are about 16.7 milliseconds apart.

    Constant frame rate Time between pictures, approximately
    24 fps 41.67 ms
    25 fps 40.00 ms
    30 fps 33.33 ms
    50 fps 20.00 ms
    60 fps 16.67 ms

    More captured pictures can describe motion more frequently. That is especially noticeable in sport, camera pans, or scrolling text. It does not automatically make each individual picture sharper: shutter exposure, motion blur, resolution, and compression still matter.

    The screen has a rate of its own

    A display's refresh rate is how often it updates, measured in hertz. A 120 Hz display can show a 24 fps film by holding each picture for five refreshes. Those extra refreshes do not contain additional recorded moments.

    If the rates do not divide evenly, some pictures may remain visible longer than others, producing an uneven cadence. Frame-rate conversion and display mode selection are different ways of dealing with rate mismatches.

    In Emby media information, the file's frame rate describes the source stream. An app or television can present it using a different refresh rate or additional motion processing. Check both when investigating judder; a smooth server delivery path does not establish a uniform display cadence.

    Also check whether the source is progressive or interlaced. A reported 25-frame/s interlaced recording can contain 50 field times per second. Treating it as 25 progressive motion samples loses an important part of its description.

    Finally, “24 fps” in a short label may actually mean 24000/1001 fps. The precise rate matters when matching timestamps and subtitles.
