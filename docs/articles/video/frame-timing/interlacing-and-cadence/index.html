    Overview: Interlacing and cadence

    Interlacing and cadence describe how fields or repeated pictures carry motion from capture to presentation.

    A field can represent a different instant from the field beside it. A telecined sequence can instead contain a pattern derived from another original frame cadence. The operation that reconstructs progressive pictures depends on which structure is present: field order, deinterlacing and inverse telecine answer different parts of that reconstruction.

    • Progressive video
      Progressive video represents each picture as a complete frame rather than fields from successive capture times.

    • Interlaced video
      Interlaced video records alternating sets of picture lines as fields captured at successive times.

    • Field order
      Field order identifies which set of alternating picture lines comes first in time.

    • Telecine and 3:2 pulldown
      3:2 pulldown distributes film pictures over alternating two-field and three-field intervals for television presentation.

    • Inverse telecine
      Inverse telecine reconstructs progressive film pictures from a telecined television cadence.

    • Deinterlacing
      Deinterlacing reconstructs complete display pictures from an interlaced video's alternating fields.
