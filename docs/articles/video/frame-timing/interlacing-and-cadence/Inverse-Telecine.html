    Inverse Telecine

    Inverse telecine (IVTC) recovers progressive film pictures from a telecined sequence. When a clean 3:2 cadence is present, it can turn a 29.97-frame-pair/s transfer back into the original 23.976 progressive pictures per second without changing the programme's duration.

    There are usually two jobs: join the fields that belong to the same film picture, then remove the redundant reconstructed picture in each five-picture cycle.

    Stage in an illustrative cycle Pictures or field pairs
    Stored telecined pairs AA, BB, BC, CD, DD
    Field matching using a suitable phase A, B, C, D, D
    Duplicate removal A, B, C, D

    Letters identify original film pictures. The matching phase can produce a different duplicate position while recovering the same four pictures.

    Recovery differs from interpolation

    When the fields match correctly, IVTC can reuse the original picture lines rather than estimating missing ones. That is an advantage over ordinary deinterlacing for a film transfer. The final encoding may still be lossy if the reconstructed pictures are compressed again.

    FFmpeg separates field matching from duplicate removal with filters such as fieldmatch and decimate. This also allows a deinterlacing fallback for portions that do not match cleanly. That implementation is an example, rather than a promise about the processing used in every Emby session.

    For a film in an Emby library, look for a regular repetition pattern and combed pairs before deciding that IVTC is appropriate. A generic 29.97 fps label is insufficient. Edits can interrupt the cadence, and an episode can combine film footage, video-shot inserts, and animated captions.

    Dropping every fifth picture without first matching fields can discard needed lines or leave combing. Applying the same operation to native interlaced video removes genuinely different capture times.

    A successful restoration should retain the programme's timing and audio synchronization, show complete pictures in moving scenes, and avoid introducing a repeated hitch at cadence changes.
