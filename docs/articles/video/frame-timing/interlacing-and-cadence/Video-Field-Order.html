    Video Field Order

    Field order tells a decoder or deinterlacer which field comes first in time. Top field first (TFF) presents the top field before the bottom field; bottom field first (BFF) does the reverse.

    “Top” and “bottom” identify alternating line sets. They do not mean the upper and lower halves of the image, and they do not determine which capture time is earlier without the field-order information.

    Correct 50-field/s example First pair Second pair
    TFF Top at 0 ms, bottom at 20 ms Top at 40 ms, bottom at 60 ms
    BFF Bottom at 0 ms, top at 20 ms Bottom at 40 ms, top at 60 ms

    Why reversed order looks like jitter

    For the TFF example, the intended motion order is 0 → 20 → 40 → 60 ms. Interpreting each pair backwards makes it 20 → 0 → 60 → 40 ms: the object repeatedly steps backward, then jumps forward. This can be more noticeable in a slow pan than in a still frame.

    Original numerical schematic using the field times from the table. Each dot is a temporal sample of steady movement; the arrows show presentation order.

    For Emby recordings, check field order when motion jitters despite an apparently adequate frame rate. A file's tag is evidence, but a wrongly tagged source can still need investigation. Inspect a moving section and compare the result under the two interpretations.

    Changing a field-order label and converting the actual field arrangement are different operations. Relabeling is appropriate when the samples are correct and only their description is wrong. A genuine conversion must handle the line sets and timing coherently.

    Progressive frames do not acquire new motion samples by adding a field-order flag. Telecined sources also need their cadence understood before field reconstruction is chosen.
