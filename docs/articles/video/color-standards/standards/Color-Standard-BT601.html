    ITU-R BT.601

    BT.601, also called Rec. 601, is a foundation of digital standard-definition television. It defines how component video is sampled and represented, including the relationship between red, green, blue, and luma/color-difference signals.

    For a media library, its most useful connection is older SD material. An archived television recording can need BT.601 color interpretation even when it is being shown on a modern 4K screen.

    BT.601 detail Why it is useful
    4:2:2 studio representation Describes horizontal sampling of luma and color differences
    13.5 MHz luma sampling for the standard 4:2:2 family Supports both 525-line and 625-line television systems
    Luma weights of 0.299 red, 0.587 green, 0.114 blue Identify the familiar BT.601 matrix
    Nominal 8-bit luma range 16–235 Black and white are not necessarily code values 0 and 255

    The luma weights apply to nonlinear R′G′B′ signals. Luma is not a direct measurement of light emitted by the screen.

    Why upscaling does not settle the color question

    Suppose an SD recording is enlarged to 1920 × 1080. Adding pixels does not by itself convert its color representation to BT.709. A converter must either retain the existing interpretation correctly or perform the appropriate color conversion and update the signal information.

    If one stage assumes BT.601 and another interprets the same color-difference values as BT.709, colored objects can shift while neutral grays still appear plausible. Increasing the bitrate cannot fix that disagreement.

    BT.601 is also not a complete shorthand for every SD source's color characteristics. Primaries, transfer characteristics, matrix coefficients, and range are separate descriptions. Old files may omit some of them. When comparing an Emby Direct Play and transcoded session, preserve the original file and compare the actual signal information before assuming the display needs a saturation adjustment.
