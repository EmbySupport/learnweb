    ITU-R BT.2020

    BT.2020, also called Rec. 2020, defines UHD television parameters, including a color gamut wider than BT.709. In media information, it is especially familiar as the color-primary label attached to HDR video.

    That label describes the encoding space. It does not promise that every scene uses extremely saturated colors or that a connected television can reproduce the entire gamut.

    Parameter BT.2020 definition
    Red primary, CIE xy 0.708, 0.292
    Green primary, CIE xy 0.170, 0.797
    Blue primary, CIE xy 0.131, 0.046
    Reference white D65
    UHD image sizes 3840 × 2160 and 7680 × 4320
    Signal precision 10 or 12 bits per component

    The gamut diagram shows how its primaries compare with P3 and BT.709. The coordinate values identify colors; they are not percentages of saturation.

    BT.2020 and HDR are separate clues

    BT.2020 itself includes a conventional transfer characteristic. HDR systems specified by BT.2100 use the same primaries with PQ or HLG. Consequently, “BT.2020” in the primaries field is insufficient to identify the brightness encoding.

    Matrix information matters too. BT.2020 defines both constant-luminance and non-constant-luminance representations. A report that says “BT.2020 non-constant” is describing the matrix, not a different gamut.

    Imagine importing two 4K files into Emby: one SDR and one HDR. Matching resolution and BT.2020 primaries would not make them interchangeable. Their transfer characteristics can require different rendering, and an SDR output may need tone mapping for the HDR file.

    Likewise, a film graded within a smaller gamut can be encoded using BT.2020 primaries. Think of the encoding space as the available coordinates, not evidence that the picture visits every corner.
