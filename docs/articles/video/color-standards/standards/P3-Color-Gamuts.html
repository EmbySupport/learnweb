    P3 Color Gamuts

    P3 identifies red, green, and blue primaries originally associated with digital cinema. Those primaries cover a wider gamut than BT.709, particularly toward saturated reds and greens. The name alone does not fully specify how picture values should be interpreted.

    The same primaries can be combined with different white points and transfer functions. These distinctions explain why “P3” on a display specification, an image file, and a mastering report need not describe identical encodings.

    Name or context What else matters
    Cinema DCI-P3 Cinema white point and a 2.6 display gamma
    P3-D65 P3 primaries with D65 white; the transfer function still needs specifying
    Display P3 D65 white and the sRGB transfer curve
    P3 colors in a BT.2020 video signal The video's declared encoding remains BT.2020

    Display P3 is explicitly defined in the W3C color specification. It is not a synonym for a PQ-encoded HDR signal. A wide-gamut photograph can be SDR.

    Reading a “P3” claim

    A television advertised as covering a percentage of P3 is describing a measured color capability, subject to the stated measurement method. It does not follow that its video input should be forced into a P3 mode for every film.

    For example, a movie can be mastered using a display with approximately P3 capabilities and delivered in a BT.2020 signal. The mastering display and the signal's primaries describe different things. Reinterpreting the encoded numbers as P3 would change their meaning.

    During Emby playback, use the file's declared color information and the actual output path together. A display's marketing gamut is not a replacement for those signal descriptions. For bright colored highlights, color volume adds another question: how much of that gamut remains available as luminance rises?
