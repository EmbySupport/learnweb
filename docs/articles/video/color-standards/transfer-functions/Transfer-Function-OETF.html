    Opto-Electronic Transfer Function (OETF)

    An opto-electronic transfer function, or OETF, converts scene-light values into a picture signal. It is associated with the capture side of the imaging chain: light measured by a camera is represented using nonlinear values suitable for a video system.

    The graph shows the reference BT.709 OETF. It rises steeply near black, allocating more signal space to low light levels than a straight linear encoding would.

    The axes show normalized scene light and signal values, not nits or raw camera sensor readings. The dashed line is a linear reference.

    For scene-light value E, the specified curve is 4.5 × E below 0.018 and 1.099 × E^0.45 − 0.099 at or above that point. At E = 0.18, the resulting signal is about 0.409. The change from a straight lower segment to a power curve matters: “gamma 0.45” alone is only a shorthand.

    The inverse is not the whole viewing experience

    An OETF is not automatically the exact inverse of the display's EOTF. The complete camera-to-screen relationship also has to produce an appropriate image appearance. The OOTF describes that overall relationship.

    This distinction is useful when processing video. Suppose a filter needs to combine light contributions. Treating an encoded value of 0.4 as 40% of scene light would start from the wrong assumption. The processing stage needs the relevant inverse function before performing operations that require linear light.

    For an Emby library, camera footage may use a conventional video transfer characteristic, HDR, or a camera-specific log encoding. The filename and codec do not identify the curve. Correct signal interpretation must survive editing and export; merely adding a familiar transfer-function label does not convert footage to that representation.
