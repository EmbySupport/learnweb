    Title disambiguation

    Title disambiguation uses type, date, people and naming context to distinguish entities that share similar or identical display titles.

    A title is a presentation label. Several unrelated works can use it, while one work can have several localized or release-specific titles.

    Similar wording creates different ambiguities

    Situation Additional evidence
    Original film and later remake share a name Release context, year and creator credits
    Film and television series share a name Entity type and series/episode relationships
    Two musical works share a title Composer, artist or recording/release context
    One film has localized titles A known shared entity and language/region relationship

    The first cases distinguish different entities. The last case can describe alternate labels for the same entity. A title-comparison operation should not treat all of them as duplicates.

    For the fictional movie candidate table in identification, a 2020 film and a 2020 series remain different even though the words and year match.

    Dates are evidence with a scope

    A film can have festival, theatrical, regional and later release dates. An album release group's original issue and a particular reissue can likewise have different dates.

    A year mismatch may indicate another work, another edition, incomplete information or a mistaken local label. Investigate its meaning before changing every record to make the strings equal.

    More precise data is useful only when it describes the same event. A file's copy date is not a more accurate version of a film's first-release year.

    Search normalization does not create identity

    Search can ignore case, punctuation or equivalent character encodings to find useful candidates. Removing a year suffix or punctuation can improve recall.

    That normalization is not proof that the results are one work. Transliteration and translation can also change wording considerably while preserving a known relationship.

    Once a match is established, a provider-scoped identifier carries a more specific reference than the displayed title. The local title can then remain localized or curated without forcing a new identity whenever the wording changes.

    In Emby, a title edit and a provider-ID correction have different consequences. Metadata Manager supplies the documented editing workflow; the evidence determines whether the required change concerns presentation, matching or both.
