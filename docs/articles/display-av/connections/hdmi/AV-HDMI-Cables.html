    HDMI cable categories

    HDMI cable categories identify tested transport capabilities and certification scopes, independently of the features implemented by connected devices.

    HDMI cable names describe transport capability and, for the relevant categories, certification. They are not a list of features that the cable can add to the connected electronics.

    The requested output mode must fit the devices and the particular cable path together.

    Official categories have different scopes

    The following summarizes HDMI LA’s public category descriptions checked in October 2026:

    Cable category Published scope relevant here
    Standard HDMI Earlier 720p/1080i applications
    High Speed HDMI Up to 10.2 Gbit/s
    Premium High Speed HDMI Certified up to 18 Gbit/s
    Ultra High Speed HDMI Up to 48 Gbit/s
    Ultra96 HDMI Up to 96 Gbit/s

    Ethernet variants and specific certification programs have their own definitions. The exact official name and certification label are more useful than a vague “4K cable” claim.

    A resolution label is incomplete because refresh, precision, chroma and link compression can change the mode’s transport requirements.

    Capability does not add device features

    A 96-Gbit/s-capable cable does not make an eighteen-Gbit/s transmitter send at 96 Gbit/s. Nor does it supply a missing HDR, VRR or eARC implementation in a connected device.

    The cable can support the requested electrical transport while the source or sink still lacks the requested operation.

    HDMI bandwidth develops that complete mode relationship. eARC also needs compatible return-audio ports and capabilities.

    The installed cable is the relevant instance

    Certification applies within its defined model/length scope. A long installation, extension, adapter or switch adds a different signal path from a short direct connection.

    An active or optical assembly can have a designated source/display direction and additional power or installation requirements. Its physical fit does not establish correct orientation.

    This does not mean every long cable fails or every short cable works. It means the full installed path should be identified.

    Compare one output request at a time

    For an Emby playback comparison, keep the same source and selected output mode while changing the cable path. That tests the transport variable more clearly than simultaneously changing resolution, refresh and HDR state.

    A path that works at one mode but fails at a higher-rate mode provides useful evidence about the requested transport. It does not prove that the movie’s compressed bitrate caused the failure.
